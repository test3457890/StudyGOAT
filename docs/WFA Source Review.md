# Provider-neutral environmental recognition review

Review date: November 13, 2026. The user does not know their WFA provider. This release therefore adds general recognition/prevention material without inferring NOLS or another provider. WFA remains partial and no provider-specific treatment qualification is claimed.

| Source | Version and selected sections |
| --- | --- |
| [CDC Preventing Hypothermia](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html) | August 25, 2026. Overview, warning signs and initial protection from continued cooling. Cool wet conditions can cause hypothermia above 40 F; confusion and coordination changes require urgent action. |
| [CDC Preventing Frostbite](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html) | August 25, 2026. Warning signs and care precautions: seek care, check for hypothermia, avoid rubbing/massage and direct high heat, avoid walking on affected feet unless necessary. Detailed field rewarming protocols are not taught. |
| [NWS Lightning Tips](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips) | Current page checked November 13, 2026. Appropriate enclosed shelter, 30 minutes after last thunder and limitations of outdoor risk reduction. A tent or isolated tree is not safe shelter. |
| [NWS Lightning Safety Overview](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-safety-overview) | Current page checked November 13, 2026. Lightning victims: no retained charge, call emergency services and provide appropriate first aid/CPR/AED assistance. Ongoing scene danger remains relevant. No CPR skill assessment is implied. |
| [CDC Yellow Book altitude chapter](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html) | 2026 edition, chapter dated April 23, 2025. Acute mountain sickness, severe cerebral/pulmonary warning signs and preventing severe illness. No higher sleeping ascent with symptoms; worsening at the same elevation calls for descent. Fitness does not eliminate risk. Confusion, ataxia and breathlessness at rest call for emergency action. Medication, oxygen dosing and diagnostic protocols are not taught. |
